The timing rule, and what today's code gets wrong

An answer's time_ms starts when the question or card is on screen and usable: after its entrance has finished, after a skip, or at the first tap or key, whichever comes first. It pauses while a flashcard turns over. Marks that play after an answer belong to the answer that was already saved, and the next question's clock never waits for them. With reduced motion the same rule holds (the shorter fades are left out too), so a student's motion setting never changes their numbers.

Found while reading the code

  • Mock Quiz (QuizRunScreen.jsx): the clock starts when Next is pressed, but AnimatePresence mode="wait" first plays the old question's 180 ms exit (the new question is not on the page yet) and then a 180 ms fade-in from transparent. Every question after the first carries about 360 ms of animation in its time_ms; the first carries its 180 ms fade-in. Keys 1–4 also work during the exit, on a question that isn't shown yet. Frame Q0 shows it side by side.
  • Flashcards (StudyScreen.jsx, FlipCard.jsx): the clock starts before the card's 180 ms fade-in, and the 420 ms flip is counted too.
  • The shuffle itself is fine: the quiz's clocks start when it ends or is skipped. It runs 1200 ms, twice the budget below.
  • Outside the budget (only fixed where a proposal touches them): Quick check animates height, the tab marker animates background-size, the loading shimmer repaints a gradient forever.

Q0 and the answer clock ship in Step 2 whatever you pick. The export codebook gets one line for time_ms in quiz_answers and flashcard_reviews, so answers before and after the fix can be told apart.

F1 The deck comes out

Where
The flashcard study screen, every time a deck starts with a card to show (not on undo, not when the deck is done or empty). The quiz no longer shuffles.
Length
600 ms: the deck splits once and riffles back. The original 1200 ms (two riffles) is beside it for comparison; it breaks the budget.
Says
"Getting your deck out." The order is not random (learning cards, then due, then new), so it never says "Shuffling".
Skip
Tap it, or Enter / Space / Escape when it has focus. Reduced motion: the deck fades in and out (150 ms each).

F2 F3 F4 A flashcard sitting

F2 paper flip
Today's 420 ms turn, plus a 4 px lift and a fold shadow that darkens the card as it stands on its edge. Off: today's flip.
F3 back into the deck
When the button you press brings the card back today (its interval is in minutes: Again, and Good on a new card), the card drops and slides under the deck's two edges.
F4 the done pile
When it goes away for days, it slides out from under the next card onto a small pile in the margin, and the count goes up. When the deck is finished the pile squares up. Undo takes it back off.
Either way
The answered card moves behind the next one, so the next card is readable from the first frame and its clock starts at once. The next card's 180 ms fade-in goes.

Q0 Next question, today and fixed

Today
The clock starts when you press Next; the old question fades out (180 ms) and the new one fades in (180 ms). The log shows when the question could first be read.
Fixed
The next question replaces the last one at once and its clock starts at its first paint. Ships whatever else you pick.

Q1 Q2 Q3 Q4 A mock quiz on exam paper

Q1 sheet handed out
After "Getting your quiz ready", the test sheet slides up 32 px into place (420 ms). Tap to skip. Off: it just appears.
Q2 marked in pen
Once the answer is saved: a pen loop round the number you chose (260 ms, pen blue), then the teacher's mark: a tick on the right answer, a line through a wrong choice (180 ms). Next works the whole time. A Yes / No question gets the loop round the Yes or No it was given. Off: today's check and cross icons.
Q3 answer sheet
One bubble per question instead of the bar; a bubble fills when its answer is saved. Never coloured right or wrong mid-quiz. Off: today's bar.
Q4 the grade
The score written large and circled in pen on the results (500 ms). Off: today's plain number.

R1 Key terms marked as you reach them

Where
The key terms list, the first time each term scrolls into view on a visit. Scroll inside the phone.
How
The marker slides in under the word (380 ms). It moves a bar with transform inside a clip, not background-size; at night it carries a dark copy of the word so the word never vanishes on the bright marker. A term that wraps onto two lines gets the mark without the slide.
Weakest of the set
Twelve terms is a lot of motion on one page. Reduced motion: the terms are simply marked.

R2 Quick check: lift the cover card

Where
The quick check after a section. The answer waits under an index card, like covering an answer with a card while you study; tapping it (or Show answer on a keyboard) slides it off (280 ms).
Why not "written in ink"
Writing out several lines of text needs clip-path or per-letter animation; neither fits the budget.
Bonus
The answer's space is there from the start, so nothing below the question jumps. Today the answer grows open with a height animation. Reduced motion: the card fades away.

S1 The week, inked in

Where
Stats, the day bars, once each time Stats opens (here: when the frame scrolls into view).
How
Each bar draws from the left like a pen stroke (scaleX, 300 ms), one day after another, 40 ms apart: 540 ms in all. The times are written from the start, so nothing waits on it.

S2 Waiting on ruled lines

Where
Explain simpler / Filipino-Taglish while the AI writes (today's grey shimmer); could also replace the quiz's spinner and "Getting your cards…".
How
Blank ruled lines; a line of ink is drawn across each in turn (520 ms each), then they clear and start again. It stops while off screen. Reduced motion: plain ruled lines and the label.

Cut

  • A page turn between Review sections: it slows reading, and every Next would animate.
  • Stamps, confetti, "New best" bursts: off the journal world, and gamified hype (PRODUCT.md, principle 3).
  • Right / wrong colours in the answer-sheet bubbles: a scoreboard in the middle of a quiz.
  • Quick-check answer written out in ink: needs clip-path on several lines of text; R2 does the job within the budget.
  • A separate "pile squares up" moment: folded into F4 as its last 200 ms.
